HOW IT WORKS

Jev Approach Control is a single-runway approach sim. It uses TypeSafe's Jev model in two ways: to turn your plain-English radio calls into typed commands, and, in Jev ATC mode, to make the controller's decisions itself. Code does the flying and the geometry. Jev only chooses from small, fixed sets of options, and every answer it gives has to meet a confidence threshold before anything happens.

Landing

A plane captures the localizer (the course that guides it down to the runway) when all of these are true at once:

  • it is cleared to land
  • altitude ≤ 3,000 ft
  • 0–11 mi east of the threshold and within 1.25 mi of the centerline
  • heading within 40° of 270

After capture it steers to the threshold, descends to 0 ft, slows to 160 kt, and lands when it is within 0.3 mi of the threshold.

Radio commands (you as controller)

Each call you type is sent once to Jev with the list of planes on frequency (callsign, spoken name such as "Speedbird 406", heading, altitude, speed). One request asks five questions:

QuestionTypeOptions
callsignChoiceevery aircraft on frequency, or none
headingChoice010–360 in 10° steps, or none
altitudeChoice1,000–10,000 ft in 1,000 ft steps, or none
speedChoice160–300 kt in 10 kt steps, or none. Relative calls ("reduce by 30") are worked out from the plane's current speed
landNoul (yes/no)probability that the call clears the plane to land

Gating: if the callsign answer's confidence is below 0.5, the reply is "say again". A heading, altitude or speed answer is applied only if its confidence is ≥ 0.5, and landing clearance only if the Noul is > 0.5. A manual heading takes the plane out of any Jev plan.

Examples
  United 701 turn heading 180, descend 4000
  Speedbird 406 reduce speed by 30
  Delta 986 cleared to land runway 27

Jev ATC mode

Every 4 s, all aircraft not yet on the localizer go to Jev in one batched request (the "speculative fan-out" pattern from TypeSafe's docs). The state includes the runway procedure, the separation rules, and for each aircraft: its current plan, altitude, speed, distance to the runway, whether it is established on final, whether it is cleared, how many approaching aircraft are ahead of it, the aircraft directly ahead (distance and speed), and the nearest traffic (distance and altitude difference).

Question (per aircraft)TypeOptions → what code does
planChoice approach → code flies it to a turn point 15 mi east of the runway and 5 mi off the centerline, then steers it onto the centerline (aiming 6 mi ahead), descending to 3,000 ft
hold → circles a holding point kept inside the radar and away from the final approach path
avoid → turns directly away from the nearest traffic and moves 1,000 ft away from its altitude
speedChoice250 no traffic ahead within 8 mi · 210 5–8 mi · 180 3–5 mi or within 10 mi of the runway · 160 < 3 mi · keep
landNoulasked only when the plane is established on final, at or below 3,000 ft and not yet cleared

Gating and guard rails:

  • A plan or speed answer is applied only if its confidence is ≥ 0.35 (JEV_ATC_CONF). Jev chooses from few options, so this threshold is lower than for radio calls.
  • A plane that has turned onto final is never switched back to holding.
  • Speed limits cap whatever Jev picks: 180 kt within 16 mi on approach, 210 kt while holding.
  • Jev's instructions are logged only when something changes, e.g. JEV → UAL701: hold present position, reduce speed 160 knots.

Why the split? When Jev was asked to pick a raw heading out of 37 options, its confidence was only 0.2–0.3. With a handful of options that describe what to do, its answers are confident enough to act on, and code keeps the geometry exact.

Usage limit

Each browser gets 5 minutes of Jev time, starting at its first Jev call, and a fresh allowance after 24 hours. The server enforces this with a signed, HttpOnly cookie. Once time is up, /api/jev answers 429 without calling TypeSafe, and the page switches off Jev ATC and the radio input. The radar keeps running. Requests over 64 KB are rejected. SESSION_MINUTES changes the allowance.

So that clearing cookies doesn't reset the allowance, there's also a per-IP rate limit: 30 requests a minute (Jev ATC makes about 15) and 300 a day, which resets at 00:00 UTC. Counters are kept in Upstash Redis when it's configured, otherwise in memory. If the limiter can't be reached, requests are refused.
